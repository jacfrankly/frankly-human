Overview (5 min): Move fast — this sets up the rest of the day.
Opening story

Meridian's mortgage app
looked seamless.

  • Upload documents, e-sign, real-time status bar
  • Behind the screen, a document still got printed and physically faxed
  • Between two departments that didn't talk to each other
  • The app was beautiful
  • The service was broken

This is what happens when design stops at the interface.

03
Story (5 min): Read it flat, let "faxed" land as absurd next to the polished UI. Don't editorialise yet.
Five problems service blueprinting solves
  • Siloed ownership — everyone owns their step, no one owns the journey
  • Invisible back-stage failures — what breaks off-screen never gets fixed
  • Unclear handoffs — the moment work passes hands is where it drops
  • Duplicated effort — three teams quietly solving the same problem
  • Misaligned incentives — teams optimising for different, sometimes conflicting goals

The interface can be perfect and the service can still fail.

04
Concept (8 min): Ask which of the five is most familiar before you finish reading the list — it primes the room to bring their own examples.
Blueprint anatomy

Five lanes.
Three lines.

Lanes:

  • Physical evidence
  • Customer actions
  • Frontstage
  • Backstage
  • Support processes

Lines:

  • Line of interaction
  • Line of visibility
  • Line of internal interaction

Everything above the line of visibility is theatre. Everything below it is the truth.

05
Concept (10 min): Draw this on the whiteboard as five horizontal lanes with the three lines running across them — participants will reference this diagram for the rest of the day.
Activity 01 · 20 min

Map the Front Stage

Activity 0120 min

Pick one real service journey you know well — something with real stakes, not a toy example.

  1. List every customer-facing step, in order
  2. Note the physical evidence at each step — email, screen, signage, object
  3. Mark where the customer is left waiting without visibility
Activity 01 (20 min): Watch for maps that only list screens — push for physical evidence too (emails, texts, printed letters, a human on the phone). If they can't name a waiting point, they haven't looked closely enough.
Diverge, then converge

Go wide.
Then go narrow.

  • Map every step first — resist the urge to fix as you go
  • Capture the whole system before judging any part of it
  • Once the map is wide, converge on the one moment that matters most
  • Diverge without judgment, converge without mercy
07
Concept (6 min): This is the same diverge/converge muscle from earlier modules — name that connection explicitly if the room needs the thread pulled through.
Mining the blueprint

Where the map
tells the truth.

  • Where do handoffs break — the exact moment work passes hands
  • Where does information get lost — reentered, rekeyed, re-explained
  • Where does no one own the moment — everyone assumes someone else does
  • Where does the fix require two teams to agree, not one team to try harder

The blueprint doesn't just show the service. It shows who's accountable for each inch of it.

08
Concept (8 min): This is the payoff slide — the blueprint is a diagnostic tool, not a documentation exercise. Land that distinction before the quick hit.
Quick hit · 5 min
"

Where in your organisation does
a handoff regularly break down?

Name it specifically — the department, the moment, the artifact that gets dropped.

Quick Hit (5 min): Take 3–4 answers from the room. Push once on vague answers ("communication is bad") — ask for the specific team and the specific artifact.
Activity 02 · 20 min

Add Back Stage

Activity 0220 min

Take the front-stage map from Activity 01. Add what's happening behind the line of visibility.

  1. Backstage — what staff or systems do that the customer never sees
  2. Support processes — the internal work (IT, ops, compliance) that makes the frontstage possible
  3. Mark the exact lane and step where it breaks — name the handoff, not "the system"
Activity 02 (20 min) — Watch for: "The system is broken" as a final answer. It never is — push once for the specific lane and the specific handoff. If they can't name it, they haven't finished mining the blueprint.
Synthesis · 10 min

The service is bigger than the screen.

Seeing the whole system, not just the interface.
Naming the specific lane where accountability disappears.
Turning "the system is broken" into "this handoff, this team, this moment."
Synthesis (10 min): This is the bridge into Module 08 — a blueprint reveals the system's structure, but structure itself is the subject of Information Architecture.
