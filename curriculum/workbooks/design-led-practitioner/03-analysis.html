Overview (5 min): Move fast — this sets up the rest of the day.
Opening story

Twenty great interviews.
Three weeks of silence.

  • Twenty interviews, all recorded, all transcribed
  • The team moved straight to "more urgent" work
  • Three weeks later, someone finally opened the folder
  • Nobody remembered which participant said what, or why it mattered
  • The insight had rotted before anyone used it

Research isn't done when the interview ends. It's done when someone makes sense of it.

03
Story (5 min): This should sting a little — most people in the room have a version of this folder. Let the silence sit before moving on.
The four-step analysis arc

Data becomes insight
in four steps.

  • Collect — get the raw material in one place
  • Sort — group by type, not yet by meaning
  • Cluster — group by pattern, theme, emotion
  • Insight — name what the pattern reveals

Skip a step and you get opinions dressed up as findings.

04
Concept (8 min): This arc recurs all day — Activity 01 is Sort + Cluster, Activity 02 is Insight. Worth writing on a whiteboard as a running reference.
Affinity mapping

Why sticky notes beat
spreadsheets.

  • One observation per note — atomic, not summarised
  • Cluster by feel first, name the cluster second
  • Physical or digital (Miro, FigJam) — the constraint is the point
  • Patterns emerge from proximity, not sorting formulas
  • Spreadsheets flatten nuance; a wall makes it visible

You can't see a pattern in a cell. You can see it on a wall.

Activity 01 · 20 min

Sort the Quotes

Six raw lines from real interview transcripts. No editing, no cleanup — sort them into emerging clusters.

  • "I just want to know if I'm on track. I don't need the graphs." — P2
  • "I checked three other apps before I trusted this one with my numbers." — P5
  • "Every time it says 'reconciling' I assume something's wrong." — P1
  • "I don't read the terms. I just look for a logo I recognise." — P4
  • "I've stopped opening it. It makes me feel behind." — P3
  • "I wanted to change one thing and ended up in four different menus." — P6
Activity 0120 min

Sort these six quotes — plus any of your own, if you're working from a real project — into 3–4 emerging clusters.

  1. Read all six once, without sorting
  2. Group by feel, not by topic — what's underneath the words?
  3. Name each cluster in 3–5 words, not a full sentence
  4. Note the one quote that resists categorising — don't force it
Activity 01 (20 min) — Watch for: Groups sorting by topic ("app quotes," "money quotes") instead of pattern. Press: "what's the feeling or behaviour underneath this one?" Pairs first, then compare cluster names as a room in the last 5 minutes.
What is an insight, actually

A quote is not
an insight.

Not:

  • A quote, however good
  • A summary of what people said
  • A statistic without a "so what"
  • A restated pain point

Is:

  • A statement that reveals something non-obvious
  • Something that explains why, not just what
  • Specific enough to point toward action
  • Often uncomfortable — it says what the team didn't want to hear

If it doesn't surprise anyone or change a decision, it's not an insight yet.

07
Concept (8 min): This is the spine of the module — land it hard. The Quick Hit and Activity 02 both test whether it stuck.
Lean personas

Personas should take
an afternoon, not a month.

  • Built from evidence you actually collected, not assumptions
  • One page, not a slide deck with a stock photo
  • Named by behaviour or goal, not by demographic
  • Disposable — rebuilt when the evidence changes
  • The point is decision-making, not decoration

A persona bloated with invented backstory is a costume, not a tool.

08
Concept (7 min): Common pushback: "but stakeholders like the polished version." Acknowledge it, then note that polish and evidence aren't the same axis — you can have both, but evidence comes first.
How Might We reframing

Turn the insight into
a question worth designing for.

  • How Might We — not "how do we," not "how can we"
  • Not too narrow (it bakes in the solution)
  • Not too broad (impossible to design against)
  • Rooted in the insight, not the feature backlog
  • A good HMW makes ten different answers possible
"

Insight: people stop checking the app because progress feels invisible.
HMW: how might we make progress feel visible without adding more to look at?

09
Concept (7 min): Walk the example slowly — it's the template Activity 02 asks people to copy with their own material.
Quick hit · 5 min
"

Write a bad insight statement.
Then rewrite it as a good one.

Bad = a summary or a quote in disguise. Good = non-obvious, points to action.

Quick Hit (5 min): Cold-call 2–3 people to read their "bad" version aloud first — it's usually funnier and more relatable than the good one, and it warms the room up before Activity 02.
Activity 02 · 15 min

Insight → HMW

Activity 0215 min

Take a real insight from your own work — the sharpest one you've got — and reframe it as a How Might We question.

  1. Write the insight in one sentence — no hedging
  2. Check it against the "not a summary" test from earlier
  3. Draft a first HMW — then widen or narrow it once
  4. Read it back — does it open possibilities, or hide a solution?
Activity 02 (15 min) — Watch for: HMWs that are really features in disguise ("How might we add a progress bar?"). Push back to the underlying human tension, not the interface fix.
Synthesis · 10 min

Analysis is a discipline,
not a phase.

The pile of notes doesn't decide anything on its own.
Sense-making is a skill — and it degrades if you wait.
An insight is only useful if it changes what happens next.
