Opening story

Luma Health shipped a
chatbot.

  • Technically correct
  • Every answer accurate
  • Emotionally off
  • 60% drop-off after the second response
  • Nobody had tested how it sounded

Language is the interface. Get the words wrong and the logic underneath doesn't matter.

03
Story (5 min): Read it flat, let the 60% land. Don't editorialise yet.
When conversation UI works

Four things have to be
true at once.

  • High ambiguity — the user doesn't have the right words yet
  • High emotion — the moment carries weight
  • High context — the system needs to remember what was just said
  • Low complexity — one thing to resolve, not a dashboard

Miss one and you don't need a conversation. You need a form.

04
Concept (8 min): This is the gate — most conversational UI fails because someone skipped this test entirely.
Dialogue design principles

Five levers, one
interface.

  • Turn-taking — who speaks first, who speaks next, no dead air
  • Response length — say the smallest true thing
  • Reading level — write for someone's worst day, not their best
  • Persona — one character, consistently, not a mood
  • Predictability — the same input gets the same kind of answer

Pull one lever and the whole conversation shifts.

Quick hit · 5 min

Have you ever spoken to an AI

Listen for pacing, brevity, and whether it admitted what it didn't know.

Quick Hit 01 (5 min): Collect 3–4 answers. Common patterns: it didn't over-explain, it used contractions, it didn't pretend to be a person.
Conversation flow design

The happy path is
the easy 20%.

  • Happy path — the case that goes exactly to plan
  • Branching — the real forks a person actually takes
  • Unexpected input — what happens off-script
  • Error recovery — how the conversation gets back on track
  • Fallback — the honest exit when nothing else works

Most teams design the happy path and call it finished. The flow is the other four.

07
Concept (7 min): This sets up Activity 01 directly — make sure "fallback" lands as different from "error recovery."
Activity 01 · 15 min

Write a Flow

Activity 0115 min

A patient messages your chatbot asking about their medication dose.

  1. Write the happy path — the bot answers clearly and confirms understanding
  2. Write one branch — a question the bot shouldn't answer alone
  3. Write one fallback — how the bot hands off without leaving the patient stranded
Activity 01 (15 min): Watch for scripts that stay clinical instead of human — press: "would you say it that way to your own mother?"
Debrief · 5 min

Where it broke.

Where did your dialogue break — and how did you design your way out?
Debrief (5 min): This is the whole point of the activity — the break is more instructive than the happy path. Have 2–3 pairs share.
Persona design

A persona is a
boundary, not a personality.

  • Voice — the words it would and wouldn't use
  • Tone — how it adjusts to the moment without losing itself
  • Character — consistent across every interaction, not just the happy ones
  • Boundaries — what it will never claim, promise, or pretend
  • Disclosure — when and how it says what it is

Personality is decoration. Persona is the interface's integrity.

10
Concept (8 min): Land hard on disclosure — it's the thread the next quick hit pulls on.
Quick hit · 5 min
"

When should a bot disclose
it's AI — and how should
it say it?

Expect disagreement — "always, upfront" vs. "only if asked." Both have real trade-offs.

Quick Hit 02 (5 min): Take 3–4 answers. Flag this for the debrief — it resurfaces in Module 10 (AI-Era Design).
Activity 02 · 15 min

Design the Failure

Activity 0215 min

Design three error states your conversational interface will hit — and the recovery response for each.

  1. Clear — the person understands what went wrong, in one sentence
  2. Human, non-defensive — never "you entered invalid data"
  3. Actionable — it tells them exactly what to do next
Activity 02 (15 min) — Watch for: recovery copy that blames the user ("invalid input," "you must") — push back to what the system will do next, not what the person did wrong.
Synthesis · 10 min

Tone isn't optional. It's the
interface.

Dialogue design is IA, copywriting, and interaction design collapsed into one.
There's no layout to hide behind — every word is a decision.
An error message is a trust test. Most bots fail it on the first line.
