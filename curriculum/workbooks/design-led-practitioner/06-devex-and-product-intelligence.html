DevEx & Product Intelligence

Internal tools are real products.
Treat them like it.

Before you start: This is a half-day, room-based module — have the Foundry deployment-tool story ready to read aloud. 02
Overview (5 min): Move fast — this sets up the rest of the day.
Opening story

Foundry's internal deployment tool took
45 minutes and 12 steps to ship a one-line fix.

  • Every engineer knew it. Every engineer worked around it.
  • Nobody in leadership knew — because nobody had ever asked
  • No usability test. No interview. No telemetry.
  • Their own engineers were treated as a given, not a user

If a customer-facing flow took 12 steps to fix a typo, someone would have been fired. This one just got quietly endured.

03
Story (5 min): Read it flat, let the number land. Ask: "Does this sound like anywhere you've worked?" — it usually does.
Who developer users are

Their reality:

  • Goals: ship safely, ship fast, avoid being paged at 2am
  • Frustrations: context-switching, undocumented steps, silent failures
  • They route around bad tools with tribal knowledge and Slack threads
  • "Just read the docs" is not a design system

The same methods apply:

  • Interviews — ask what they actually do, not what the runbook says
  • Usability tests — watch someone attempt a real task, unscripted
  • Journey maps — the deploy pipeline is a journey like any other
  • Developers are users. They are not exempt from research.

The tools get worse specifically because everyone assumes engineers will just cope.

04
Concept (8 min): The key reframe: "developer user" is not a lesser category of user. Land it before moving on.
DevEx principles

Four things every internal
tool should respect.

  • Reduce cognitive load — don't make people hold state in their head
  • Fast feedback loops — the gap between action and consequence should be seconds, not minutes
  • Clear error states — a failure with no explanation is a design failure
  • Documentation as a design surface — docs are UI, not an afterthought

These are the same principles good product design has always used. Nothing here is DevEx-specific — it's just applied to a different user.

The product intelligence loop

Telemetry → Pattern → Hypothesis
→ Experiment → back to telemetry.

  • Telemetry — instrument the tool, see what's actually happening
  • Pattern — where do people stall, retry, abandon, escalate?
  • Hypothesis — a specific, falsifiable guess about why
  • Experiment — a small, scoped change to test the hypothesis
  • Back to telemetry — did it actually move the pattern?

Without telemetry, every internal tool decision is a guess wearing a suit.

07
Concept (7 min): This is the thesis for the Intelligence Questions activity — draw the loop on the whiteboard if presenting live.
Activity 01 · 15 min

Intelligence Questions

Activity 0115 min

Write three questions your team could answer if you had better usage telemetry on one internal tool.

  1. Pick one real internal tool — the one from the Quick Hit works well
  2. Write three questions telemetry could actually answer, not opinions telemetry can't settle
  3. For each, note what data point or event you'd need to capture
  4. Pairs — compare and sharpen each other's questions — 5 minutes
Activity 01 (15 min): Watch for questions that are really just opinions in disguise ("is the UI ugly?") — press toward things an event log could actually answer ("how many retries before success?").
Internal product design

Same rigor. Different
audience.

  • Personas — engineers have goals, contexts, and skill levels too
  • Journey maps — trace the full deploy/build/debug path, not just the happy path
  • Usability testing — sit with someone while they actually use the tool
  • The rigor gap is a status gap, not a methodology gap

"It's just for engineers" is the internal-tools version of "it's just a B2B product" — a permission slip to skip the work.

Which DevEx principle does your

Cognitive load, feedback loops, error states, or documentation.

Quick Hit 02 (5 min): Take 3–4 answers from the room. Note the pattern — it usually feeds directly into Activity 02.
Activity 02 · 20 min

Research Plan

Activity 0220 min

Sketch a lightweight research plan for improving one internal tool. Not a full research plan — a scrappy, doable one.

  1. Who will you talk to or observe, and how many?
  2. What will you watch them do — a real task, not a demo?
  3. What telemetry, if any, already exists — and what's missing?
  4. What's the smallest version of this you could run in one week?
Activity 02 (20 min) — Watch for: "I'll just ask engineering what they want." That's not a research plan — it's a shortcut that reproduces the exact blind spot from the Foundry story. Push for an actual observed task or interview.
Synthesis · 10 min

Treating internal tools as real products.

Developers are users. Their frustration is data, not noise.
Telemetry turns "I think" into "I know" — and hypotheses into experiments.
The research methods don't change. Only the audience's job title does.
Synthesis (10 min): This is the bridge into Module 07 — service design extends this same "back stage matters" logic to the whole organisation, not just engineering.
