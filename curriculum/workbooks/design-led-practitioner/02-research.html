02
Overview (5 min): Move fast — this sets up the rest of the day.
Opening story

Luma Health built a medication
reminder app for patients.

  • Built on assumptions about what patients needed
  • Thoughtful notifications, clean UI
  • Nobody had interviewed a patient who'd actually missed a dose
  • It launched
  • It flopped

This is what happens when you build for imagined users, not real ones.

03
Story (5 min): Read it flat, let the last line land. Don't editorialise yet — the reframe comes next.
The two research traps

Research theatre:

  • Interviews that confirm what you already believe
  • Leading questions dressed as curiosity
  • A deck nobody reads
  • Insights that arrive after the decision's made

Research paralysis:

  • "One more round" before we can decide
  • Endless discovery, no synthesis
  • Confidence mistaken for more data
  • A team too afraid to commit

Both are avoidance. Just dressed differently.

04
Concept (8 min): Ask the room which trap they recognise in themselves — most people have a default. Naming it is the point, not fixing it yet.
Qual vs quant

Two different questions.
Not competing tools.

  • Qual tells you why — the story behind the number
  • Quant tells you how many — the scale of the pattern
  • Qual without quant: a compelling anecdote you can't size
  • Quant without qual: a number you can't explain
  • Most real decisions need both, in sequence

Quant tells you where to look. Qual tells you what you're looking at.

05
Concept (8 min): Land hard on the closing line — it's the one participants tend to write down verbatim.
Interviewing 101

The craft is listening,
not asking.

  • Open questions — "tell me about the last time…," not "would you use a feature that…?"
  • Follow the energy — where they lean in, dig there
  • Silence is a tool — let the pause do the work
  • Don't lead the witness — your job is to hear their reality, not confirm yours
06
Concept (8 min): Demonstrate one bad question live ("Wouldn't it be great if the app reminded you?") and ask the room to fix it on the spot before moving to Activity 01.
Activity 01 · 15 min

Write 5 Questions

Activity 0115 min

Pick a real project you're working on. Write 5 interview questions you'd actually ask a user.

  1. Individual — write 5 open questions, no leading
  2. Aim for questions about behaviour, not opinion
  3. Keep them in front of you — Activity 02 comes back to these
Activity 01 (15 min): Watch for closed questions ("would you use…?") and questions with the answer baked in. Don't fix them yet — that's Activity 02.
Seven biases that corrupt research
  • Confirmation bias — hearing what you already believe
  • Leading questions — the answer is baked into the ask
  • Social desirability bias — people tell you what sounds good
  • Recency bias — the last thing you heard weighs the most
  • Sunk cost — too invested in the plan to hear it's wrong
  • Groupthink — the room converges before anyone's tested it
  • Curse of knowledge — you can't imagine not knowing what you know
08
Concept (8 min): This is the spine of the Quick Hit and Activity 02 that follow — don't rush it, but don't lecture either. One line per bias is enough.
Quick hit · 5 min
"

Which of these seven biases have
you fallen into recently — and
how do you know?

Naming it is the first move — most researchers can't spot it in themselves in the moment.

Quick Hit (5 min): Take 3–4 answers from the room. Watch for people naming someone else's bias, not their own — press gently: "and you, specifically?"
Activity 02 · 15 min

Fix the Questions

Activity 0215 min

Return to your 5 questions from Activity 01. Rewrite any that are leading, closed, or biased.

  1. Mark each question: open or closed, leading or clean
  2. Rewrite the ones that fail
  3. Pairs — trade and pressure-test each other's rewrite — 5 minutes
Activity 02 (15 min) — Watch for: rewrites that just soften the wording but keep the lead ("Don't you think…" → "What do you think…" is not a fix if the frame is still yours). Push for structurally open.

The real work of research.

It's not the interview. It's the listening.
Bias doesn't announce itself — you have to go looking for it.
