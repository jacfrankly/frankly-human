Opening story

Meridian Bank briefed a designer
to build a new savings product.

  • No clarity on revenue model
  • No clarity on cost to serve
  • No named customer segment
  • The designer built something beautiful
  • It made no commercial sense — and never shipped

Good design on top of no business model is still a bad decision.

03
Story (5 min): This is a different failure mode from Module 01's Meridian story — that one skipped research, this one skipped viability. Name the difference if someone asks.
The Business Model Canvas

Nine blocks. One page.
How the business actually works.

Customer & value:

  • Customer segments
  • Value propositions
  • Channels
  • Customer relationships
  • Revenue streams

Infrastructure & cost:

  • Key resources
  • Key activities
  • Key partnerships
  • Cost structure

If you can't fill in all nine blocks for your project, you don't understand the business yet.

04
Concept (10 min): Osterwalder's canvas. Don't teach all nine in depth — the point is that revenue and cost sit right next to customer and value on the same page. Designers usually only see the right side.
The Value Proposition Canvas

Zoom into one block:
customer fit.

Customer profile:

  • Jobs — what they're trying to get done
  • Pains — what frustrates or blocks them
  • Gains — what outcome they actually want

Value map:

  • Products & services — what you offer
  • Pain relievers — how it removes the pain
  • Gain creators — how it produces the gain

Fit is when the value map actually answers the customer profile — not when it looks good next to it.

05
Concept (10 min): Also Osterwalder — pairs with the BMC's "value propositions" block. Emphasise fit is earned, not assumed: most decks match these up cosmetically, not causally.
Activity 01 · 20 min

Fill the Canvas

Activity 0120 min

Fill out a Value Proposition Canvas for a real product or feature you're currently working on.

  1. Individual — name the product or feature
  2. List the customer's jobs, pains, and gains — from evidence, not assumption
  3. Map your product's pain relievers and gain creators against them
  4. Pairs — test the fit out loud — 5 minutes
Activity 01 (20 min) — Watch for: Jobs/pains/gains written from the team's assumptions, not real evidence. Press: "how do you know?" DO NOT fill the canvas for them.
Jobs to Be Done

People don't buy products.
They hire them.

  • A customer "hires" a product to do a job
  • Functional — the practical task it completes
  • Emotional — how they want to feel doing it
  • Social — how they want to be seen doing it
  • Fire it the moment something does the job better

Christensen's milkshake: not "tastes good" — "keeps me occupied and not hungry on a boring commute."

07
Concept (8 min): Clayton Christensen. The milkshake study is the classic example — use it if the room needs a concrete anchor before the quick hit.
Quick hit · 5 min
"

What job is your current project
actually being hired to do?

Not the feature. The job underneath it — functional, emotional, or social.

Quick Hit (5 min): Collect 3–4 answers. Watch for feature-language answers ("it's a dashboard") — push toward the job ("it's hired to make someone feel in control before a board meeting").
Activity 02 · 20 min

Write the Recommendation

Activity 0220 min

Write a one-page strategic recommendation that connects a real user need to a real business outcome.

  1. State the user need in one sentence — evidence-based, not assumed
  2. State the business outcome it drives — revenue, cost, retention, or risk
  3. Recommend the decision — what should happen next
  4. Name the trade-off you're accepting
Activity 02 (20 min) — Watch for: Recommendations that stay purely user-need-framed with no named business outcome — that's the exact failure from the opening story. DO NOT let a recommendation skip the business outcome. DO NOT write it for them.
Synthesis · 10 min

The real work of strategy.

Not a deck. Not a framework for its own sake.
Connecting what a human needs to what a business can sustain.
A designer who can't make that connection gets talked over by someone who can.
Synthesis (10 min): This is the bridge into Module 05 — product thinking is how strategy becomes an operating rhythm, not a one-off document.
