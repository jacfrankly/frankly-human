02
Overview (5 min): Move fast — this sets up the rest of the day.
Opening story

Orbit's content library grew
for three years.

  • 400+ pieces of content and counting
  • Untitled, unstructured, uncategorised
  • Nobody could find anything
  • Including the team that built it
  • Structure had never been designed — it just accumulated

Nobody chose this mess. Nobody chose anything — that was the problem.

03
Story (5 min): Read it flat. Let "including the team that built it" land — it's the punchline. Don't editorialise yet.
What IA actually is

Not menus.
Not sitemaps.

  • Organising — grouping things so they make sense together
  • Labeling — naming things the way people actually say them
  • Structuring — deciding what sits above, beside, and below what
  • Navigating — giving people (and now AI agents) a way to find what they need

Menus and sitemaps are the artifacts. IA is the thinking underneath them.

04
Concept (8 min): Land hard on "IA is invisible when it works" — that's the spine of the whole module.
Mental models

Design for how they
think — not how you
store it.

  • The org chart is not a navigation model
  • The database schema is not a navigation model
  • Users don't know your team structure and shouldn't have to
  • Structure should mirror the user's task, not your system's architecture

Orbit's library was organised by "who uploaded it." Nobody outside that team could ever guess that.

05
Concept (8 min): This is where people usually admit their own product is organised by internal team, not by user task. Let the discomfort sit for a second.
Classical IA methods
  • Sitemaps — the shape of the whole structure, one level at a time
  • Taxonomies — the categories and hierarchy content lives inside
  • Card sorting — let real users group real content, watch what they cluster
  • Tree testing — give users a task, watch where the structure fails them

These methods are old. They still work — because the underlying problem hasn't changed.

06
Concept (8 min): Quick pass — the activity that follows is where this becomes real, not theoretical.
Activity 01 · 20 min

Card Sort

Activity 0120 min

Take a real set of content or features — yours, your team's, or the provided placeholder set. Sort it into groups that make sense to you, not to whoever built it.

  1. Individual — write out 15–20 real items (pages, features, content types)
  2. Group them into categories that feel obvious, not clever
  3. Name each group the way you'd say it out loud, not the way engineering would
  4. Pairs — compare groupings and discuss where they diverge — 5 minutes
Activity 01 (20 min): Watch for groupings that mirror the org chart or the CMS folder structure — press: "would a first-time user say it that way?" Divergence between pairs is the point, not a failure.
Agent-era IA

There's no menu.
Structure moved inside.

  • Conversational and agentic interfaces have no visible sitemap
  • The structure now lives in decisions: what to ask, when, and in what order
  • What the system assumes vs. what it confirms with the user
  • When it hands control back — and how clearly it says so

IA didn't disappear when the menu did. It just stopped being visible.

08
Concept (8 min): This is the module's real pivot — from "arranging things a user sees" to "arranging decisions a system makes." Give it room.
The interruption model

Three modes.
You have to choose.

  • Interrupt — stop and ask before proceeding
  • Act autonomously — proceed without asking, report after
  • Wait — hold and do nothing until the user initiates

Every agentic feature makes this call somewhere. Most teams never make it on purpose.

09
Concept (8 min): This is the module's core deliverable framework — make sure the three modes are on the wall or slide visibly before Activity 02.
Quick hit · 5 min
"

Think of an AI tool you use — when
does it interrupt you, and does that
timing feel right?

Use this to surface how often interruption timing is accidental, not designed.

Quick Hit (5 min): Collect 3–4 answers from the room. Common complaint: tools interrupt too often for low-stakes things and not at all for high-stakes ones. Use it to set up Activity 02.
Activity 02 · 20 min

Design the Interruption Model

Activity 0220 min

Take a real or hypothetical AI feature. Define three specific rules — one for interrupt, one for act autonomously, one for wait.

  1. What condition triggers an interruption? Be specific — not "when it's unsure"
  2. What condition allows it to act without asking?
  3. What condition means it should do nothing until the user initiates?
  4. For each rule, name what happens if you got the boundary wrong
Activity 02 (20 min) — Watch for: "Always interrupt" or "never interrupt" as a rule — these aren't rules, they're the absence of one. Press for the specific condition. Reversibility and stakes are usually the real variable underneath.
Synthesis · 10 min

Structure you can't see is still structure.

Every unlabeled folder is a decision nobody made on purpose.
Every AI interruption is a decision, whether you designed it or not.
IA didn't get smaller in the agent era. It got harder to see.
