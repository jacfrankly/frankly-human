AI-Era Design

AI changes what designers build.
It doesn't change why.

02
Overview (5 min): Move fast — this sets up the rest of the day.
Opening story

Meridian's AI loan advisor is
accurate 87% of the time.

  • Fast, confident, well-designed screens
  • Demoed beautifully — every time
  • The 13% failure doesn't show up in the demo
  • It shows up three weeks later, in an email from another team
  • Nobody had designed for what happens when it's wrong

AI errors aren't edge cases. They're system errors — and design owns the system.

03
Story (5 min): Read it flat, let the 87%/13% split land. Don't rush to the reframe — let the room sit with it.
What changes with AI

Same discipline.
New failure mode.

  • Outputs are probabilistic, not deterministic
  • Confidence varies by input, not just by system
  • Reasoning is often opaque — even to the team that built it
  • Errors compound silently across a session
  • Trust becomes the interface

You're not designing an answer. You're designing a relationship with an answer that might be wrong.

04
Concept (8 min): Land hard on "trust becomes the interface" — it's the spine of this module.
The transparency spectrum

Black box:

  • Confident output, no reasoning shown
  • Fast, but unaccountable
  • Fails silently
  • Users can't calibrate trust

Explainable:

  • Shows its working, even briefly
  • Slower, but auditable
  • Fails visibly, with a path forward
  • Users learn when to double-check

The right point on the spectrum is a design decision, not a technical default.

05
Concept (8 min): Ask the room where their own product currently sits on this spectrum — most will guess wrong.
Quick hit · 5 min
"

Name a product that uses AI —
does it tell you when it's uncertain?

Most don't. That's usually the tell.

Quick Hit 01 (5 min): Common answers: autocomplete, recommendation feeds, chatbots. Push: "did it say maybe, or did it just say it?"
Confidence UI

Design for
uncertainty, not just answers.

  • Show ranges, not single numbers, when the model has a range
  • Use confidence levels — high / medium / low — not fake precision
  • Separate "best guess" from "certain," visually, every time
  • Let people ask "how sure are you?" without digging for it
  • Silence reads as certainty — say something instead

A confident-looking wrong answer is worse than an honest maybe.

07
Concept (7 min): This is the practical toolkit for the activity that follows — slow down here.
Activity 01 · 15 min

Design a Trust Signal

Activity 0115 min

Pick an AI feature you know — real or hypothetical. Design the trust signal that goes with its recommendation.

  1. What does the interface show to build trust? (source, confidence, reasoning)
  2. What does it deliberately not show — and why?
  3. Write the exact phrase the AI uses when it's uncertain
  4. Pairs — trade and stress-test each other's signal — 5 minutes
Activity 01 (15 min) — Watch for: teams defaulting to "just show a percentage" — press for the actual phrase, not just the number. Debrief prompt: "What did you choose to show — and what did you choose to hide?"
Trust patterns

The patterns that hold up
when it's wrong.

  • Progressive disclosure — reasoning available, not forced
  • Reasoning previews — a glimpse of "why," not a wall of logs
  • Graceful failure — a wrong answer that still leaves you oriented
  • Human handoff moments — built in, not bolted on after a complaint
  • Audit trails — so "the AI did it" is never the end of the sentence

These are UX patterns. They're also accountability patterns.

09
Concept (7 min): Tie back to Meridian — which of these five would have caught the 13% before it reached Compliance?
Quick hit · 5 min
"

In an AI-assisted decision — who is
responsible when the AI is wrong?

There's always an answer. Most organisations haven't said it out loud.

Quick Hit 02 (5 min): Take 3–4 answers. Expect deflection to "the model" — push back: a model doesn't sit in a performance review.
Activity 02 · 15 min

Map the Handoff

Activity 0215 min

Take an AI-assisted flow you know. Map the four moments: where AI acts alone, where it pauses, where it asks, where it hands back control.

  1. Acts alone — no human in the loop, ever
  2. Pauses — waits for a signal before continuing
  3. Asks — explicitly requests a human decision
  4. Hands back — control returns to a human, fully
Activity 02 (15 min) — Watch for: "the AI just handles it" used as the answer for every stage — that's not a handoff, that's an abdication. Press for the specific moment control changes hands.
