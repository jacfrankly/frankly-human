Opening story

Three teams tackled
the same problem.

  • Team A ran research, wrote it up, handed it to product — never saw it applied
  • Team B jumped straight to strategy — skipped research, moved fast, solved a symptom
  • Team C ran the whole loop — research → insight → strategy → design → test — and looped back when the data didn't add up
  • Only Team C found the root cause
  • The difference wasn't skill in any one discipline — it was integration across all of them

Same brief. Same deadline. Same budget. One team connected the dots.

03
Story (5 min): Read it flat. Let the room sit with "same problem, different outcome" before you name why. Don't editorialise yet — the next slide does that.
The integration challenge

Research → insight → strategy → product
→ service → data → judgment.

What most teams do:

  • Over-index on the discipline they're most comfortable in
  • Skip steps under deadline pressure
  • Lose the thread between research and the decision it was meant to inform
  • Treat each discipline as a handoff, not a conversation

What integration requires:

  • Holding the whole chain in view, not just your link in it
  • Knowing when to loop back, not just move forward
  • Translating between disciplines, not just doing your own well
  • Treating judgment as the connective tissue, not a final gut-check

Every module you've done this program is a discipline. Integration is what makes them a practice.

Concept (8 min): This is the thesis of the whole capstone module — everything after this builds on "integration is the differentiator." Land it before moving on.
The Design-Led Loop

One pattern, run
on repeat.

  • Frame — name the real problem, not the presenting one
  • Learn — go find out what's actually true
  • Make sense — turn evidence into insight
  • Decide — choose a direction, with judgment, not just data
  • Design — give the decision a shape
  • Test — find out where you're wrong before it's expensive
  • Integrate — connect it back to the system it lives in
  • Repeat — the loop doesn't end, it tightens

This isn't a new framework. It's the eleven modules before this one, in order.

Cross-discipline synthesis

Where the magic
actually happens.

  • Evidence — what did research and data actually show?
  • Opportunity — what's the strategic upside, honestly sized?
  • Feasibility — what can we build and operate, for real?
  • Risk — what breaks, and who carries it if we're wrong?
  • Judgment — given all of the above, incomplete as it is, what do we decide?

Synthesis isn't averaging five opinions. It's holding five kinds of evidence in tension until a decision falls out.

07
Concept (8 min): Distinguish synthesis from consensus — consensus waters down, synthesis sharpens. This is the skill the Capstone Challenge is about to test.
Activity 01 · Capstone Challenge · 45 min

Run the Loop

Activity 0145 min

Choose a real organisational problem — yours, or one the group agrees on. Run a compressed version of the Design-Led Loop against it, in groups of 3–4.

  1. Frame — write the real problem in one sentence, not the presenting symptom
  2. Learn — name what you'd actually go find out, and from whom
  3. Analyse — what would that evidence probably tell you?
  4. Strategise — what's the direction it points to?
  5. Design — sketch the shape of the response, in words or a rough diagram
  6. Test — name the riskiest assumption and how you'd check it fast
  7. Recommend — write the one-paragraph recommendation you'd bring to a stakeholder
Activity 01 (45 min): This is the loudest, messiest activity of the whole program — let it be. Circulate and press groups that skip straight to Design without a real Frame. 45 minutes is tight for 7 stages; tell them speed and honesty beat completeness.

Designing your practice

Rituals sustain what
a workshop can't.

  • Weekly research — a standing habit, not a one-off project
  • Insight reviews — evidence gets revisited, not filed and forgotten
  • Decision briefs — the reasoning gets written down, not just the outcome
  • Blueprinting sessions — the whole service gets seen together, regularly
  • OST updates — the opportunity space stays current, not static
  • Data check-ins — the numbers get looked at before they become a surprise

A ritual is a decision you only have to make once. That's how integration survives past this room.

Activity 02 · The Capstone · 40 min

Your Operating Manual

This is the capstone of the entire 13-module program — not just today. Write the document you'd hand a new hire, or your future self, so the way you practise design doesn't live only in your head.

Activity 02 · Capstone of the Program40 min

Write your Design-Led operating manual — your version, not a template filled in.

  1. Principles — the two or three things you now believe about how design decisions get made well
  2. Rituals — the practices you're committing to, and how often
  3. Methods — the two or three tools from this program you'll actually reach for
  4. Commitments — what you're promising your team, and yourself
  5. Anti-patterns — the specific ways you've watched yourself skip a discipline under pressure, named plainly
Activity 02 (40 min) — the true capstone: Give this real room to breathe — don't compress it to make time elsewhere. Individual, mostly silent writing; resist the urge to make it a group exercise. This document is theirs to keep, not to present. If time allows, invite one voluntary share of a single line, not the whole manual.
