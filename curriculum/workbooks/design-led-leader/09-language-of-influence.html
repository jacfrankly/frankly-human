Opening story · draft

Three meetings.
One decision. Zero words.

  • Head of Design at Marlow Health, a mid-size digital health platform
  • Watches a roadmap risk build across three consecutive leadership meetings — she's seen the pattern in user data for weeks
  • Says nothing each time. Not asked directly. Doesn't want to be the person who speaks out of turn
  • Third meeting: leadership commits next quarter's budget to the exact path her data warned against
  • She realises the invitation to speak was never coming — the room doesn't know to ask for an opinion it doesn't know she has

Being right in your notebook and being right in the room are not the same thing.

You have the opinion. You're waiting to be asked.
  • The Silent Expert — has the strategic opinion, has learned not to offer it uninvited
  • Waiting to be asked isn't humility. It's a habit built somewhere it once made sense to build it
  • Somewhere along the way, "unsolicited" became a synonym for "overstepping"
  • Breaking the habit isn't about volume — it's about building a script for offering an opinion well

Priya wasn't wrong. She was quiet. The room only ever learns from the second one.

Activity 01 · 20 min · Draft

Translate the Recommendation

Activity 0120 min

Take one real design recommendation you've made or want to make. Translate it into three short versions — same core point, three different rooms.

  1. Individual — write the recommendation as you'd naturally say it to a design peer
  2. Write a finance version, a sales version, an engineering version — 1–2 sentences each
  3. Pairs — read your engineering version aloud to a partner. Would it actually land in that room, or does it still sound like a design memo?
Activity 01 (20 min) — DRAFT: Watch for translations that just swap in a business-sounding word without changing the underlying logic ("improves usability" restated as "improves ROI" isn't a translation). Push for what that specific audience actually tracks. This activity is invented to fit the sourced "translate into three vocabularies" outcome; not itself sourced from the docx.
Unsolicited doesn't have to mean overstepping
  • Name why you're speaking now — "I wasn't asked, but this connects directly to what we just decided"
  • Lead with the stakes, not the opinion — what's at risk if the room doesn't hear it
  • Offer it as a contribution to the decision, not a correction of the room
  • Keep it short enough that it doesn't need permission to exist

A script isn't a speech. It's two or three sentences you've already rehearsed once, so you're not composing them live.

Activity 02 · 20 min · Draft

The Unsolicited Opinion Script

Activity 0220 min

Write a personal script — 2–3 sentences — for offering a strategic opinion in a room without being asked.

  1. Individual — name a strategic opinion you're currently sitting on, unasked, right now
  2. Write the opening line first — how you'd frame why you're speaking, so it doesn't read as a correction
  3. Write the opinion itself, stated plainly, in one or two sentences after it
  4. Pairs — say it out loud to a partner. Does the opening line sound like a memo, or a person?
Activity 02 (20 min) — DRAFT: Watch for scripts that skip straight to the opinion with no opening line — that's the tell it'll land as a correction, not a contribution. This activity is invented to fit the sourced "build a personal script" outcome; not itself sourced from the docx.

A script rehearsed once beats a good opinion composed live and lost.
