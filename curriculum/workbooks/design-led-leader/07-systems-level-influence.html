Opening story · draft

The Renewal Experience sat in
a seam for four months.

  • Priya Nandakumar, Director of Design at Solterra Health, a mid-size benefits company
  • The Renewal Experience initiative touched Member Experience (design's home) and Claims Operations (owned the underlying data and timing rules)
  • Member Experience called it a timing problem. Claims Ops called it a UX problem. Neither VP would claim it
  • Biweekly syncs happened for four months. Nothing shipped — there was no single owner in the room who could say yes
  • Priya started sending the recap notes herself. Tracking the open decisions herself. Convening the next sync herself — without asking either VP for authority over the other's team
  • Six weeks later, the seam had a workable plan. She'd never been granted orchestration. She'd claimed it

Nobody promoted Priya into that seam. She just stopped waiting for someone to.

Two systems, running at once

The formal system

  • The org chart, the reporting lines
  • The steering committee that's listed on the slide
  • The approval workflow as documented
  • What a new hire is told on day one

The informal system

  • Who actually needs to be pre-briefed before a room
  • Who the real sponsors are
  • Whose quiet "no" kills a decision the org chart says they don't own
  • Where the org chart quietly lies about where power sits

Service design's real leadership use isn't mapping the formal system — it's reading the informal one: who actually needs to be pre-briefed before a room, who the real sponsors are, and where the org chart quietly lies about where power sits.

Where the seams live
  • Practitioner equivalent: Service Design — but the leadership use of it is different from the practitioner one
  • Your formal boundary stops at your function's box on the chart. The outcome your customer feels doesn't
  • This is the module that extends influence beyond the design function's formal boundaries
  • It builds two threads at once — Relationship Capital and Executive Trust — because holding a seam well requires both
  • It breaks the Silent Expert trap (you stop waiting to be invited into someone else's seam) and loosens the Service Department Trap (you're no longer the last function pulled in after the seam's already been decided elsewhere)

Activity 01 · 15 min · Draft

Map the Seam

Activity 0115 min

Identify a real cross-functional outcome in your own org that currently has no clear owner, and sketch who touches it.

  1. Individual — name one outcome your customer or the business feels, that sits across two or more functions
  2. List every function that touches it, and mark which ones think it's "someone else's job"
  3. Pairs — compare seams and discuss what's kept each one unclaimed — 5 minutes
Activity 01 (15 min) — DRAFT: Watch for participants naming a seam that's actually just "another team is slow" — press for a seam where ownership itself, not just execution, is genuinely ambiguous. This activity is invented to fit the framework; not sourced from the docx.
Orchestration is not authority
  • Convene before you're asked — send the recap, not a request for permission to send one
  • Track the decisions nobody else is tracking. Become the record of truth for the seam
  • Make it easy for both sides to keep saying yes to you convening, and awkward for either to say no once you already are
  • You're not overriding either function's calls — you're removing the gap between them

Priya never asked Claims Ops to report to her. She just made sure the seam had a record, a rhythm, and a next step — every time.

Activity 02 · 20 min · Draft

The Sponsorship Map

Activity 0220 min

For one real, upcoming decision, map the informal sponsorship network — who actually needs to say yes before the room — against the formal org chart.

  1. Individual — name one real decision you need a room to say yes to in the next month
  2. List who formally approves it, per the org chart
  3. List who actually needs to be pre-briefed and say yes first, and in what order — even if they're not formal approvers
  4. Pairs — compare the two lists and discuss where they diverge, and what that divergence tells you — 8 minutes
Activity 02 (20 min) — DRAFT: Do not map the sponsorship network for a participant — this is theirs to build. If a story turns into naming a colleague's bad behaviour, redirect to the structural pattern: what does the gap between formal and informal tell them about how this org actually decides. Synthesis · 10 min

The seams are where the biggest wins live, and they're political.

A seam nobody owns isn't a gap to escalate. It's an opening to claim.
Orchestration isn't authority — it's showing up with the record, the rhythm, and the next step.
The org chart tells you who's accountable. It rarely tells you who actually has to say yes.
