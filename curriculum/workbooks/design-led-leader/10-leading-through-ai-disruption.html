The core claim

Define your own value proposition
— before someone else defines it for you.

  • Every function is being asked to justify itself against AI. Design is no exception
  • The default posture is reactive — wait to be asked what AI changes, then answer
  • The alternative is proactive — walk into the conversation with the definition already made
  • Silence isn't neutral. It's someone else writing the definition for you

Budget Authority + Roadmap Influence

AI puts two threads under
direct pressure.

  • Budget Authority — the thread most literally on the table when someone models "how many designers do we need"
  • Roadmap Influence — the thread that decides whether design shapes what's next, or is told what AI already decided
  • Both threads erode the same way: through absence from a conversation, not through a single bad decision
  • This module treats AI as the sharpest current test of the threads named in Module 01, not a new framework

The threads don't change under AI pressure. What changes is how fast the erosion happens if you're not in the room.

Anticipate the argument, don't wait for it
  • Every AI capability announcement becomes ammunition for a headcount conversation, whether or not anyone in the room means it maliciously
  • The people modelling it usually aren't being adversarial — they're doing their job with the information they have
  • If design isn't in that modelling conversation, someone else supplies the assumptions
  • Pre-empting means naming the argument yourself, before it's made about you

Activity 01 · 15 min · Draft

The Shrinking Argument

Activity 0115 min

Write the strongest possible case for shrinking your own design function using AI — argue it like you mean it.

  1. Individual — write the real argument a CFO or CEO could make for a smaller design team, using AI capability as the justification. No strawmanning — if it wouldn't convince the room, it's not finished
  2. Circle the single assumption the argument depends on most heavily — the one that, if untrue, the whole case collapses
  3. Pairs — swap arguments. Your partner's job is to test whether the assumption you circled is genuinely the weakest one, or just the most comfortable one to attack
  4. Note which version — yours, or your partner's read on it — actually survives
Activity 01 (15 min) — DRAFT: Watch for a strawman — if it's easy to knock down, it's not the real argument. Press: "would this actually convince your CFO?" This activity is invented to fit the framework; not sourced from the docx.
Position, don't react

A roadmap already assumes
something about your team.

  • Every AI-augmented roadmap already contains an assumption about what design contributes — whether or not design wrote it
  • Reacting means responding to that assumption after it's shipped in a planning document
  • Positioning means supplying the assumption before the document is written
  • The difference isn't tone. It's timing

Quick hit · 5 min
"

What's one thing AI genuinely
cannot do that your function
still holds?

Not what you hope is true. What you'd actually defend under a hard follow-up.

Quick Hit 02 (5 min): Take 3–4 answers from the room. Note which answers are craft-shaped versus judgment-shaped — it previews Activity 02.
Activity 02 · 15 min · Draft

Position the Function

Activity 0215 min

Draft one paragraph positioning what your function uniquely contributes to an AI-augmented roadmap — written to be said before you're asked.

  1. Individual — name what AI genuinely accelerates for your team, and what it still can't do — judgment, taste, synthesis across ambiguity, whatever is true for you specifically
  2. Anchor it to the roadmap, not to craft — this is about where the function sits in what's next, not a defense of the work itself
  3. Write it as an opening line for a conversation you haven't had yet, not a rebuttal to one you've already lost
  4. Pairs — read aloud, cut anything that sounds defensive rather than proactive
Activity 02 (15 min) — DRAFT: Watch for defensive framing sneaking back in ("we still matter because…") — that's the tell it's still a rebuttal, not a proactive statement. Never write it for them.
Synthesis · 10 min

The function that defines itself first isn't reacting to the argument. It is the argument.

Someone is already modelling your function's future. The only question is whether you supplied the assumptions.
Budget Authority and Roadmap Influence erode through absence, not through one bad decision.
Anticipate the argument. Position the function. Do both before you're asked.
