Opening story · draft

She won the seat.
Eighteen months later, it was gone.

  • A design leader fought for eighteen months to earn a standing seat at the roadmap table, and real budget authority to go with it
  • She got both. The org chart changed. Her sponsor announced it in an all-hands
  • No recurring ritual held either one in place — no standing check-in, no non-negotiable about what she'd walk away from
  • Eighteen months later, a reorg quietly folded the seat back into someone else's remit. Nobody decided to take it — it just wasn't defended by anything but her

One win isn't a system. A system is what's still standing when the person who built it takes a two-week holiday.

Twelve modules. One system.

Four threads. Three traps.
One practice.

The four threads

  • Budget Authority — shaping spend, not just executing it
  • Roadmap Influence — shaping what's next, not reacting to it
  • Executive Trust — in the room when strategy is decided
  • Relationship Capital — who sponsors you, who owes you, who you owe

The three traps

  • The Service Department Trap — executing decisions made elsewhere
  • The Feature Factory Manager — measured on output, not outcomes
  • The Silent Expert — has the opinion, learned not to offer it

This isn't new content — it's Module 01, and every module since, in one place. You didn't come here to learn a fourth trap. You came here to make what you've already built hold.

Activity 01 · 20 min · Draft

Write Your Manifesto

This is not a values poster. It's the short list of things you will hold even when it costs you something — written in your own words, tested against a real memory of pressure, not an aspiration.

Activity 0120 min

Write 3–5 non-negotiables you will hold regardless of organisational pressure.

  1. Individual, silent writing — write what's actually true, not what sounds impressive in a room
  2. For each non-negotiable, name the specific pressure that would tempt you to break it
  3. Cross out any line you haven't actually held under real pressure at least once — rewrite it honestly, or cut it
  4. Pairs — read one non-negotiable aloud, not the whole list — 5 minutes
Activity 01 (20 min) — DRAFT: Give this real room — it's the emotional on-ramp to the 90-day plan, don't compress it to save time elsewhere. Watch for manifestos that read as generic leadership-poster language ("integrity," "empathy") — press once for the specific pressure that tests it. Activity 02 · The Capstone · 30 min

Your 90-Day Plan

This is the capstone of the entire 13-module program — not just today. Name the thread and trap most true for you, the first real move, whose sponsorship carries it, and the ritual that keeps it standing at day 90 and beyond.

Activity 02 · Capstone of the Program30 min

Write a 90-day plan for your lever and trap — not a template filled in.

  1. Name the thread and trap most true for you right now — from your diagnostic, or your own honest read across this program
  2. Name the first concrete move — not a theme, an actual action with a date attached
  3. Name whose sponsorship you need, and how you'll ask for it in the next two weeks
  4. Map your current sponsorship — who sponsors you today, who you sponsor, and who's gone quiet
  5. Name one ritual — recurring, not one-off — that holds this in place past day 90
  6. Pairs — read your ritual aloud, not the whole plan — 5 minutes
Activity 02 (30 min) — the true capstone: Give this the most room of anything in the program — don't compress it to protect time elsewhere. Mostly individual, mostly quiet writing; resist making it a group exercise. This document is theirs to keep, not to present in full — if time allows, invite one voluntary share of a single line, not the whole plan.
