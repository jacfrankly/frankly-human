Concept · Identity

Identity is a story,
not a fact

Identity is:

  • A story
  • A pattern
  • A set of behaviours
  • A design material

Common inherited identities:

  • The fixer
  • The achiever
  • The strong one
  • The responsible one
Ask: "Does any of that feel familiar?" Don't take answers yet — sets up Quick Hit 01. | Gently: "Most of us are living from a story that was written before we could choose."

Quick Hit · 5 min
"

Which identity still clings to you even though it's no longer true?

Write it down. Don't edit it.

Timer: 60 seconds silent writing · 2 min pair share · collect 3–4 from the room. | Common answers: the helper, the overachiever, the peacekeeper, the perfectionist. | Land on: "Outdated identities are adaptations — not flaws."
Concept · Outdated Identity

It once served you

Outdated identities were once useful. They:

  • Protected you
  • Helped you belong
  • Helped you succeed

But an identity built for a previous chapter can limit the next one.

Ask: "What did your outdated identity protect you from?" Take 2–3 examples. | Redirect anyone being harsh on themselves: "This identity helped you survive something — what's the strength inside it?"
Concept · Emerging Identity

Who you're
becoming

Emerging identity is already showing up in small ways.

It's not aspirational. It's directional.

Look for it in your impulses, your discomforts, and the moments you surprise yourself.

Set up Activity 01. | If people struggle: "Where have you recently done something that felt more like the 'new you' than the 'old you'?"
Activity 01 · 20 min
Activity 01 — Individual + Pairs + Group 20 min

Current Identity → Outdated Identity → Emerging Identity

  • Write: "I am someone who…" — your current identity in one sentence
  • Name your outdated identity — the role or pattern you're outgrowing
  • Write: "I am becoming someone who…" — your emerging identity
  • Pairs (6 min): share one sentence — the one that feels most true
  • Group (2 min): "What surprised you?"
Watch for: Job titles → redirect to behaviour. | Apologising for outdated identity → reframe as adaptation. | Aspirational emerging identity → "Where does this already show up, even in small ways?"
Debrief 01 · 10 min

Choose one to
explore

Which identity feels most active right now?

What part of your outdated identity is hardest to let go of?

Where does your emerging identity already show up?

Land on: "Identity shifts slowly — but naming it accelerates the shift." | Let the room sit with this before the break.

Concept · Identity Friction

Old patterns fighting
new behaviour

Identity friction shows up as:

  • Hesitation before a new behaviour
  • Guilt when you stop playing the old role
  • Overthinking a decision that should feel easy
  • Self-doubt at the threshold of change

It's not a warning sign. It's the sign of a transition.

Old identity
"I must be useful"
→
Friction
guilt when resting
→
Emerging identity
"I honour my energy"
Ask: "Where does your old identity fight your new one?" | Sets up Activity 02. | Normalise: "Friction is evidence that the shift is real — not evidence that something's wrong."
Activity 02 · 20 min
Activity 02 — Individual + Pairs + Group 20 min

Map Identity Friction + Choose a Behaviour Bridge

  • Identify one place your old identity fights your new one
  • Write the behaviour your emerging identity would choose
  • Shrink it to the smallest version that still counts
  • Pairs (6 min): share the behaviour bridge
  • Group (2 min): "What made that behaviour feel important?"
Watch for: Behaviours too big → "What's the 5% version?" | Self-criticism → "Transition is hard — what's one gentle step?" | Keep actions grounded, not aspirational.
