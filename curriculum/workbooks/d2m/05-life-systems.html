Module 05 · Overview

What this module
is about

  • The four life systems — Work, Relationships, Self, Environment
  • System friction — where repeated breakdowns happen
  • Leverage points — small shifts that create big change
  • System redesign — one intentional shift
Frame: "Your life is not random. It's systemic. Every system you live inside produces predictable outcomes — including the ones you don't want." | Connect to Module 04: energy leaks often live inside broken systems.
Concept · Systems

A system is a
pattern with outputs

Systems are made of:

  • Patterns — what repeats
  • Inputs — what feeds it
  • Constraints — what limits it
  • Feedback loops — what it produces

Your life has four core systems. When one system is out of alignment, it affects the others.

The system with the most friction is usually the one asking for attention.

Ask: "Which system feels most out of alignment lately?" Don't take answers — sets up Quick Hit 01. | Keep language neutral: systems aren't about blame, they're about design.

Quick Hit · 5 min
"

Which system in your life is producing the most friction?

Write it down. Don't edit it.

Timer: 60 sec silent writing · 2 min pair share · collect 3–4 from the room. | Common answers: work overload, relational tension, self-neglect, chaotic environment. | Land on: "Friction is information. It tells you where the system is breaking."
Concept · Four Systems

Your four core
systems

Work System
Demands, expectations, role clarity, boundaries, culture, output vs capacity
Relationship System
Support, reciprocity, emotional labour, care, conflict, belonging
Self System
Identity, habits, self-talk, energy management, inner life, personal rituals
Environment System
Home, digital spaces, physical context, routines, objects, sensory inputs
Ask: "Which system feels most stable? Which feels most neglected?" Take 2–3 examples. | Watch for: people focusing only on work — invite all four.
Concept · Friction

Friction is
information

Friction shows up as:

  • Repeated breakdowns — the same problem, again
  • Chronic stress — not acute, but constant
  • Misalignment — doing what doesn't fit who you are
  • Overwhelm — more inputs than the system can process

Friction is rarely random. It's patterned — and redesignable.

Set up Activity 01. | Redirect self-blame: "This is about the system design — not about you as a person." | Watch for: people naming only surface symptoms — ask what's underneath.
Activity 01 · 20 min
Activity 01 — Individual + Pairs + Group 20 min

Map your Four Systems

  • For each system — write what's working and what's not
  • Notice patterns across systems — what shows up in more than one?
  • Identify the system producing the most friction right now
  • Pairs (6 min): share one system that feels most alive or most broken
  • Group (2 min): "What pattern showed up across multiple systems?"
Watch for: Self-blame → "This is about system design, not personal failure." | Only naming work → invite all four. | Minimising relational or environmental friction → normalise it.

Land on: "The system that produces the most friction is the one asking to be redesigned." | Let this sit before the break — people often have a moment of recognition here.

Concept · Leverage

Small shifts,
big change

Leverage points aren't overhauls. They're pressure points.

Example leverage points
Stop checking email after 7pm
→
What it changes
Rest, mental load, presence

Other examples: move your desk · say no to one recurring obligation · add one weekly ritual · eat before the hard conversation.

Ask: "What's one small shift that would change everything?" | Sets up Activity 02. | Normalise: "We're not redesigning your whole life — we're finding the one pressure point that matters most right now."
Activity 02 · 20 min
Activity 02 — Individual + Pairs + Group 20 min

System Friction → Leverage Point → System Shift

  • Choose the system with the most friction
  • Identify the leverage point — the smallest shift with the biggest impact
  • Choose one system shift to implement this week
  • Pairs (6 min): share the shift
  • Group (2 min): "What made that shift feel meaningful?"
Watch for: Shifts too big → "What's the 5% version?" | Self-blame → "We're redesigning the system, not fixing you." | Trying to fix other people → "What's within your influence?"
Debrief 02 · 10 min

Choose one to

What value does this shift honour?

What constraint does this shift respect?

Land on: "You don't need a new life. You need a redesigned system." | This is a powerful close to the first half of the program — let it land fully.
