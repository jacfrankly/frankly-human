Not a motivation problem

Most people don't have a
motivation problem.

They have a decision design problem. Your life is the sum of the decisions you make over and over:

  • What you say yes to
  • What you say no to
  • What you tolerate
  • What you avoid
  • What you prioritise
"

Good decisions aren't about certainty. They're about clarity.

03
Concept (8 min): Read the frame slowly: "Your life is the sum of the decisions you make over and over." Land on the core claim before moving on — write it on the board if you can. | Watch for: people who hear this as pressure. Reframe: "This isn't about being perfect. It's about being clear."
Decision patterns

Every decision runs on a
pattern.

  • Autopilot — made without thought, on repeat
  • Inherited — made by someone else's rules
  • Fear-based — made to avoid a feared outcome
  • Avoidance — the decision not to decide

None of this is a character flaw. Patterns can be redesigned.

04
Concept (10 min): Read each pattern aloud slowly. Ask: "Which one runs your decisions most often?" — don't take answers yet, this sets up Quick Hit 01. | Watch for: people who self-diagnose harshly. Reframe: "Naming the pattern is the redesign."

Decision hygiene

Clarity lives in the
separation.

Most bad decisions happen because five things get tangled together:

  • Facts
  • Interpretations
  • Assumptions
  • Fears
  • Values

Separate them, and the decision usually gets simpler — even if it doesn't get easier.

Activity 01 · 20 min

Decision Hygiene

Activity 01 20 min

Choose one real decision you're facing. Sort it into what's fact, what's interpretation, what's assumption, what's fear, and what's values.

  1. Individual — 14 minutes silent writing
  2. Pairs — share one thing that surprised you — 6 minutes
  3. Group — "What changed when you separated fact from interpretation?"
Activity 01 (20 min) — Watch for: People who conflate fact with interpretation ("They don't respect me" is not a fact) · People who skip the fear column because it feels exposing · People who can't name a value because they've never been asked. | Reframe: "The fear is data. It just isn't the whole picture."

Choose one.

"Which fear showed up loudest — and was it true?"
"What value were you protecting without realising it?"

The decision matrix

Weigh what actually
matters.

Once a decision is clean, evaluate the real options against five things:

  • Cost
  • Benefit
  • Energy impact
  • Value alignment
  • Future-self alignment

Activity 02 · 20 min

Design your
next right move

Activity 02 20 min

Choose one decision — the same one, or a new one. Design the next micro-step. Small enough to actually do.

  1. Individual — 12 minutes: shrink the decision to its smallest next step
  2. Pairs — share your next right move — 6 minutes
  3. Group — "What made it feel doable?"
Activity 02 (20 min) — Watch for: Moves that are still too big — shrink again. People who want to solve the whole decision today — redirect to the single step. People who pick a "safe" move to avoid the real one — gently name it. | What good looks like: concrete, small, dated.
Debrief 02 · 8 min

Choose one.

"What made this move feel doable rather than daunting?"
"What would have to be true for you to actually do it?"
"What's still uncertain — and can you move anyway?"

You don't need certainty to move. You need clarity and a small enough step.

Debrief 02 (8 min): Choose one question. Land on: "You don't need certainty to move. You need clarity and a small enough step." | If questions are still open: "Write them down — they're for you, not for now."
