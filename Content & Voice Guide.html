02 · Two Registers, One Practice

Editorial. Earthy.

Same anchor. Different rooms. The editorial register goes to the boardroom and the workshop. The earthy register goes to the kitchen table. Pick one per artefact. Don't mix mid-document.

Register A · Editorial

Show up.
Make it land.

For: DESIGN-LED · Service Blueprinting · LinkedIn · Programme decks

Soft white paper, sharp ink, monospace labels, single bright accent. Magazine-grade type. Big confident numbers. One idea per slide. No drop shadow, no gradient, no filler.

Register B · Earthy

Mind yo'self.
Take your time.

For: Design to Me · Workbooks · Reflection prompts · Newsletters

Soft sand paper, warm charcoal, more white space, hand-drawn moments, lower-case section titles. Olive and clay accents. Slower rhythm. The same backbone — just gentler in the room.

The kit.

A small, reusable kit. Every component earns its place by raising signal, never by adding noise.

Dot · title · meta

07 · Content & Voice

How it sounds.

Warm clarity with a backbone. Short sentences. Strong verbs. Specific numbers. Named scenarios. No filler. Take a position.

Six patterns that recur

Pattern 01
Two-part declaration
"Research isn't a phase. It's a habit."
Pattern 02
Specific provocation
"Meridian Bank shipped on time. Drop-off at step 2 was 34% worse."
Pattern 03
Parallel contrast
"Feature teams own roadmaps. Empowered teams own outcomes."
Pattern 04
List that earns it
"Three parts: intro, body, conclusion."
Pattern 05
Direct instruction
"Write 5 questions. No yes/no. 12 minutes."
Pattern 06
Honest debrief
"Which question had the solution hidden inside it?"

Good · Bad

Good · headline

Structure is invisible until it breaks.

Two-part declaration. Specific verb. Raises the stakes. Reads in one breath.
Bad · headline

The Role of Information Architecture in Product Design

Topic heading, not a claim. Capitalisation imposed. No verb. Could be the title of any deck.
Good · provocation

Meridian Bank's exec team gets a 47-slide pack every Monday. Decisions still get made on gut feel. Nobody knows which number to trust.

Named org. Specific number. Names the failure. Stops at the failure — the lesson comes later.
Bad · provocation

Many organisations struggle with data overload, which can make it difficult for leaders to make informed decisions.

Vague subject. Hedge ("can make it difficult"). Reads like a textbook chapter opener. Solves nothing.
Good · activity

Write 5 interview questions. Rules: no yes/no, no solution embedded, at least one probes emotion. 12 minutes.

Imperative. Numbered constraint. Time stated. Trusts the participant to do the work.
Bad · activity

In this activity, you'll have an opportunity to explore how you might approach writing some questions.

Hedge spiral. No constraint. No time. The instruction softens itself out of existence.
08 · What we don't do

No filler. No tells.

If a thing makes the work look or sound like it came from a corporate template, an AI default, or a stock template — cut it. The list:

×
Wordsleverage · drive outcomes · holistic approach · best-in-class · gain valuable insights · designed to help · in today's fast-paced world · empower (verb)
×
PunctuationExclamation marks. If it isn't exciting, punctuation won't fix it.
×
TypeDisplay serifs. Italic display headings. Helvetica. Hand-drawn body copy. Anything below 24 px on a slide.
×
ColourCorporate blue. Purple-to-blue gradients. Three accents fighting on one slide. Pure black on pure white.
×
EffectsDrop shadows. Heavy gradients. Glow. Bevel. Reflection. Anything from 2008.
×
LayoutCentred body copy. Six bullets fighting for attention. A title that's a complete sentence about the topic.
×
ImageryStock photos with smiles. Hand-drawn SVG attempts at complex illustration. Emoji as decoration.
×
PatternsHumble-brag posts. "Unpopular opinion:" openers. Lists of ten things. Filler like "great question."
