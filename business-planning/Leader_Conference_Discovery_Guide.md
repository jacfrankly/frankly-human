# Design-Led: Leader — Conference Discovery Guide

**Purpose:** This is research, not sales. You're pressure-testing the four-thread framework (Budget Authority, Roadmap Influence, Executive Trust, Relationship Capital) and the three traps (Service Department, Feature Factory Manager, Silent Expert) against what Heads of Design actually describe — not pitching a program. Nothing here has a price tag on it this week.

Don't lead with "I'm building a program." Lead with genuine curiosity. If someone asks what you're working on, there's a bridge line at the end — use it, don't open with it.

---

## How to bring it up naturally

You don't need a formal opener. Real conversations at a conference already drift toward "how's work" — from there:

- "Honestly? Good work, not enough influence. You ever feel that?"
- "I've been thinking a lot about why good designers still don't get the room. Does that resonate, or is your org different?"
- If they mention politics, frustration, or being overruled unprompted — that's an opening, not an interruption. Follow it.

---

## The core questions (use 2–3, not all of them — this is a conversation, not a survey)

**On budget and roadmap:**
- Do you shape what gets built next, or find out after it's decided?
- When did you last get looped into a budget conversation before it was already settled?

**On executive trust:**
- Are you in the room when strategy actually gets set — or briefed on it afterward?

**On relationship capital (the one most people don't expect to be asked):**
- Who actually sponsors you in this org? If you had to name one person, could you?
- Has a weaker idea ever won because of who was behind it rather than what it was?

**On the traps — listen for which one they reach for unprompted:**
- "Brought in to execute a decision already made" → Service Department Trap
- "Measured on velocity, not outcomes" → Feature Factory Manager
- "Had the opinion, didn't say it" → Silent Expert

---

## What you're actually listening for

- Does the framework match their language, or are they reaching for different words? If several people independently describe something the current three traps don't capture, that's more valuable than confirmation.
- Is Relationship Capital landing as "obviously true" or as "interesting, hadn't thought of it that way"? Both are useful signal, but they mean different things about how central to make it.
- Notice if someone's org is *not* political — that's useful contrast, not a wasted conversation.

---

## Capture notes (do this same day, while it's fresh)

For each conversation worth remembering, jot:

- **Who** (role, org type — no need for names if it feels off)
- **Which trap** they reached for first, unprompted
- **Exact phrase** they used, if it was sharp — real language beats your paraphrase every time
- **Anything that didn't fit** the framework as built

---

## If they ask what this is

Only if asked directly — don't volunteer it:

> "I'm building out a leadership program for Heads of Design — the pattern I keep seeing is people with real credibility who still can't get in the room. Still shaping it, actually — this conversation's helpful."

That's it. No pitch, no waitlist ask this week. The follow-up connection request after the conference is where the actual next step happens.
