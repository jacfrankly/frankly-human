## The anchor

> **Warm clarity with a backbone.**

Grounded, human, modern, systems-literate, emotionally intelligent. Never corporate. Never cold. Never generic "design-system."

- **Warm** — human, not clinical. Acknowledges the work is hard. Earns warmth through specificity and honesty, doesn't perform it.
- **Clarity** — one idea at a time, short sentences, strong verbs, no unearned jargon. Test: could a non-designer understand this on first read?
- **Backbone** — doesn't hedge. Takes a position. Says "this is wrong" when something is wrong.

## The six core sentence patterns

1. **The two-part declaration** — short declarative, then the harder truth. *"Research isn't a phase. It's a habit."* The second sentence raises stakes, isn't a synonym.
2. **The specific provocation** — a real story, specific numbers, a named (fictional, for Design-Led) organisation. Ends on the failure; the lesson comes later.
3. **The parallel contrast** — two versions of the same thing that reveal a gap. *"Feature teams own roadmaps. Empowered teams own outcomes."*
4. **The list that earns its existence** — only when items are genuinely parallel and independent. Max six, usually three or four. Don't bullet a paragraph that should just be written.
5. **The direct instruction** — imperative, numbered, specific, time-boxed. No "you might consider."
6. **The honest debrief question** — never "how did that go?" Always has a specific edge, always in quotes on slides, never answered for the reader. *"Which question had the solution hidden inside it?"*

## Writing rules

- Short sentences — more than two clauses, cut in half. Active voice, strong verbs.
- Em dashes (—) encouraged as pivots, not comma replacements. Contractions are fine.
- One idea per paragraph; body paragraphs max four sentences.
- Numbers: numerals for data, always with context (*"34% worse — not slightly worse, not meaningfully worse. 34%."*). Round numbers are suspicious.
- Oxford comma yes. No exclamation marks. Ellipses only when a pause earns it.

## What to avoid

- **Corporate consultant register**: "designed to help participants leverage...", "drive outcomes", passive construction.
- **AI-generated enthusiasm**: "Great question!", "In today's fast-paced world...", "At the end of the day..."
- **The hedge spiral**: "It might be worth considering whether perhaps..."
- **The passive insight**: "Research has shown that..." — rewrite as a direct claim.
- **The vague emotional claim**: "This is a powerful experience that will transform..." — show it, don't claim it.
- **Jargon without payoff**: banned outright — *ecosystem, leverage (verb), synergies, holistic approach, digital transformation journey, best-in-class*. Allowed only if immediately unpacked: *empowered teams, continuous discovery, opportunity solution tree, service blueprint, vibe coding*.

## LinkedIn / marketing specifically (STYLE_GUIDE §08)

**Voice:** professional but human, commercially confident, never corporate bland.

**Does not do:** humble-brag formatted as insight, thread-bait false tension, "Unpopular opinion:" openers, "10 things you need to know" lists.

**Does:** short observations with a sharp point, real questions she actually has, specific things she's noticed that others haven't named yet.

**[ADDITION]** Ends on observation, not a call-to-action — this specific rule isn't spelled out in STYLE_GUIDE.md's LinkedIn section (which describes what *not* to do) but is a standing Frankly Human content rule. Apply it as the default for organic posts; CTAs belong in email, not organic social.

## Track-specific register — the Leader/Practitioner split **[ADDITION, inferred]**

STYLE_GUIDE.md §03 and §10 treat "DESIGN-LED" as one register (senior designers and design leads, sharp edge, business scenarios). But the Design Leadership Diagnostic and Design Thinking Diagnostic docs (`leader_extract.txt`, `business-planning/_dtd_curriculum_extract.txt`) are explicit that **Design-Led: Leader is pitched higher than Design-Led: Practitioner** — "less warmth-first framing," "this audience wants to be taken seriously fast," "more 'here's what I see, tell me if I'm wrong,' less 'let's explore this together.'" Practitioner keeps STYLE_GUIDE's baseline DESIGN-LED register (direct, commercially fluent, "a light wink"); Leader drops the wink and moves faster to precision. This distinction is not yet in STYLE_GUIDE.md — treat it as inferred from the diagnostic docs until it's folded back into the canonical guide.

## Scenario organisations (Design-Led content only)

Always name a fictional org, always concrete named failure, never an individual's fault. Canonical set (STYLE_GUIDE §11): **Meridian Bank** (retail bank), **Luma Health** (digital health), **Cargo** (B2B logistics SaaS), **Orbit** (consumer streaming), **Foundry** (internal platform team). Do not invent new ones without reason — reuse these for consistency across posts.
