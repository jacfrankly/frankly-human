# Curriculum Context — Design-Led: Leader

Track: **Design-Led: Leader**. Source: this session's `scratch_curr2.txt` (full curriculum extract) and `leader_extract.txt` (the diagnostic extract) — both extracted from `curriculum/design-led-leader/Design_Led_Leader_Curriculum.docx` and the Design Leadership Diagnostic doc respectively. All content sourced directly; nothing invented.

**13 half-day modules — the executive counterpart to Design-Led: Practitioner.** Where Practitioner teaches judgment inside a project, Leader teaches judgment inside an organisation — with the politics, budget, structure, and relationships that come with it.

**The point of view** (also see `Design_Led_POV_Manifesto.md` — the flagship POV piece): Designers who build real product management capability — and get recognised for it — build stronger, more credible leadership careers than designers who don't. **Capability** is actually doing product thinking under real constraints; **credibility** is being recognised as capable of it by the people who decide who gets the seat. Most advice for designers only ever addresses credibility (better language, positioning) without capability. Why now: AI has compressed the execution layer of design and product at once — what's left is judgment, and judgment doesn't respect the old "design's job" / "product's job" boundary.

**The core claim:** organisational influence runs through four threads, not three (as the Practitioner-facing diagnostic frames it) — the fourth is added specifically for Leader:

1. **Budget Authority** — shaping spend, not just executing against someone else's
2. **Roadmap Influence** — shaping what's next, not reacting to what's already decided
3. **Executive Trust** — being in the room when strategy is decided, not briefed on it after
4. **Relationship Capital** — who sponsors you, who owes you, who you owe, whether that map is current

The first three assume influence follows formal process; Relationship Capital is the thread for organisations where it doesn't. Most design leadership content avoids naming office politics directly — this curriculum names it deliberately.

**The three traps** (every module names which thread it strengthens and which trap it helps break):
- **The Service Department Trap** — brought in to execute a decision already made elsewhere
- **The Feature Factory Manager** — measured on velocity and output, not outcomes or strategy
- **The Silent Expert** — has the strategic opinion, has learned not to offer it uninvited

**Entry point:** Design Leadership Diagnostic — $500, 20–30 minute AI-coached conversation or paid 1:1 intake. Explicitly flagged in the source doc as a *proposed, unvalidated* framework — "a draft to pressure-test, not a finished spec," to be checked against real client stories before it ships. Opens with an organisational-power story (a Head of Design's design system hit 90% adoption; she still wasn't in the room when the company set its three-year strategy). Produces a **Leadership Influence Profile** — e.g. "You've built the credibility. You haven't been given the room."

## The 13 modules

Each module states its Practitioner equivalent — same topic, played at the executive table instead of the project table.

| # | Module | Practitioner equivalent | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | The Business Case for Design | Foundations | The AI-compressed-judgment thesis; diagnose which of the four threads is weakest in your own function. |
| 02 | Research as Organisational Intelligence | Research | Redirect research toward strategic, not tactical, questions. Strengthens Executive Trust. |
| 03 | Making the Insight Undeniable | Analysis | Board-ready one-page narratives; pre-empt objections. Addresses the Silent Expert trap. |
| 04 | Owning the Roadmap | Strategy | Real prioritisation rep — score three competing initiatives against scarcity, defend the sequence. Strengthens Roadmap Influence. |
| 05 | From Service Function to Product Partner | Product | Names the exact handoff point where the function gets relegated to execution. Directly addresses the Service Department Trap. |
| 06 | Investing in Internal Capability | DevEx & Product Intelligence | Real payback-period maths on a real investment case. Strengthens Budget Authority; names the Feature Factory Manager trap. |
| 07 | Systems-Level Influence | Service Design | Mapping the *informal* sponsorship network, not just the org chart. Builds Relationship Capital + Executive Trust. |
| 08 | Structuring How the Business Decides | Information Architecture | Diagram how a real decision actually gets made, including informal parts. |
| 09 | The Language of Influence | Conversational UI | Translate a recommendation into finance/sales/engineering vocabularies. Breaks the Silent Expert habit. |
| 10 | Leading Through AI Disruption | AI-Era Design | Define the function's value proposition before someone else defines it. Applies Budget Authority + Roadmap Influence to the sharpest current threat. |
| 11 | Metrics That Win the Room | Data & Decisions | A real North Star metric tree tracing design decisions to business outcomes. Strengthens Executive Trust + Budget Authority. |
| 12 | Executive Decision Hygiene | Judgment in Practice | Decision hygiene at board level — and the honest limit: process improves the call, doesn't guarantee it wins on merits vs. sponsorship. |
| 13 | The Operating System for Design Leadership | Capstone / Integration | Personal leadership manifesto, rituals, a 90-day plan returning explicitly to the participant's own diagnostic result. |

Four modules carry real reps rather than simulations (the curriculum is explicit about this "capability, not just credibility" design principle): Module 04 (real prioritisation call), 05 (real sequencing decision), 06 (real payback-period maths), 11 (real North Star metric tree).

## Voice notes specific to this curriculum

- Pitched higher than Practitioner — see `positioning.md` and `brand-voice.md` for the full register distinction. "Skip the warm-up." "This audience will discount anything that sounds like generic leadership-coaching language."
- Guardrail language (for the diagnostic specifically): if a participant becomes cynical/defeated, redirect to a specific moment rather than accepting "nothing will ever change here" as settled. If they want to name-and-blame a specific executive, redirect to the structural pattern. Distress/burnout signals get an immediate, direct check-in, never brushed past.
- The four-thread/three-trap framework is explicitly marked as unvalidated in the source doc — pressure-test language ("proposed," "worth checking against real client stories") should stay in internal/planning content about this curriculum, but doesn't need to leak into public-facing marketing copy, which can state the framework directly.
