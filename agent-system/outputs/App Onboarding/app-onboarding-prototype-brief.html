3 — Provisional triage criteria (fast-track)

These are provisional criteria for the prototype. They will be replaced with criteria formally defined and signed off by Cybersecurity and Legal (D-02) before the pilot. Every criterion must be answerable from a verifiable source — not requester self-assessment.

Design rule: The intake form must never ask "Is this app low risk?" The form asks factual questions. The triage engine classifies. If any criterion cannot be verified from a document or system, the app routes to standard review automatically.

# Fast-track criterion (ALL must be true) How verified Status
C1 Vendor appears on the organisation's approved vendor list System lookup PLACEHOLDER
C2 No integration with financial, HR, or identity management systems required Intake form field PLACEHOLDER
C3 No personal data processed or stored (employee, customer, financial, or health data) Intake form field PLACEHOLDER
C4 SaaS / cloud-hosted with SOC2 or ISO27001 certification Intake form field PLACEHOLDER
C5 Estimated annual licence cost under £5,000 Intake form field PLACEHOLDER

If any criterion is false or unknown → standard pathway. If all five are true → fast-track classification presented to requester as "estimated 7–10 business days, subject to review."

5 — Hypotheses and pass/fail criteria

These replace the Experiment Designer stage. The prototype is the experiment. Pass/fail must be evaluated before making any changes to the design — do not iterate during testing.

H1 App discovery reduces duplicate submissions TD-01
Hypothesis
If the app catalogue is searchable by function and plain-language description, requesters will find and check existing apps before submitting a new request — without being prompted to do so.
Pass criterion
≥70% of test participants navigate to the catalogue independently and check it before proceeding to the intake form.
Fail criterion
Participants skip the catalogue, cannot find results for apps that exist in it, or proceed directly to intake without checking.
What to watch for
Do participants search by app name or by function? If by name only, the catalogue search design needs rethinking. Are placeholder app descriptions clear enough to match a business need?
H2 Front door produces complete submissions TD-02
Hypothesis
If intake form fields are scoped to what reviewers actually need, with clear help text explaining why each field is required, submissions will arrive complete — reviewers can act without chasing additional information.
Pass criterion
Reviewer test participants can act on ≥80% of test submissions without requesting additional information.
Fail criterion
Reviewers identify missing or ambiguous fields they would need to chase in ≥50% of submissions.
What to watch for
Which fields cause confusion for requesters? Which fields are reviewers most likely to find incomplete? Gap between what requesters think they've answered and what reviewers actually need.
H3 Confirmation screen reduces status-chasing TD-02 / TD-03
Hypothesis
If requesters receive a clear confirmation with a reference number, the names of teams reviewing, and an honest SLA range (7–30 business days), they will tolerate waiting without escalating — even though the range is wide.
Pass criterion
In post-test debrief, participants describe the confirmation as "enough to wait" and say they would not chase or escalate within the first week.
Fail criterion
Participants say they would email, call, or escalate regardless of the confirmation — or that the SLA range is too wide to trust.
What to watch for
Is the 7–30 day range too wide to be reassuring? Does naming the six reviewing teams make the process feel transparent or bureaucratic? Would a tighter range for fast-track (e.g. 7–10 days) change behaviour?
H4 Triage self-declaration produces unreliable classifications TD-02 triage
Hypothesis
Requesters will describe their apps in ways that produce fast-track eligibility — even when the objective criteria would route to standard review — if any criterion relies on their own risk assessment rather than a verifiable fact. This is the hypothesis we want to validate as real, so we can design against it.
Pass (risk confirmed)
≥30% of test participants describe their app in a way that produces a fast-track classification that a reviewer participant would overturn on inspection.
Fail (risk not found)
Participants consistently answer triage questions accurately and reviewer participants agree with the classifications.
How to test this deliberately
Include one or two test scenarios where the app should clearly route to standard pathway. Observe whether the triage questions guide the requester to the correct answer, or whether the framing allows optimistic interpretation. Compare requester classification against a reviewer's independent assessment of the same submission.

6 — Outstanding actions before pilot launch

Action 1 — Critical (blocks pilot, not prototype)
Name the service owner. A Director-level individual with formal authority over all six teams (App Packaging, Supplier Governance, Legal, Cybersecurity, Licence Management, SSO) must be appointed and communicated to all team leads before the pilot goes live. Without this, channel decommissioning cannot be enforced and the service will fragment. This requires a decision at leadership / sponsorship level — it cannot be resolved by the programme team alone. Raise at next steering group or executive sponsor meeting.
Action 2 — Required before intake form is finalised
Run D-01: six-team data-mapping exercise. Convene a session with all six reviewing teams to agree the definitive intake form field set. Use the provisional field set in Section 4 as the starting point. Fields marked REFINE are most likely to change. The service owner (once named) should chair this session.
Action 3 — Required before fast-track goes live
Run D-02: Cybersecurity and Legal governance alignment session. Produce a formally agreed, written definition of fast-track eligibility criteria using verifiable, non-self-declared fields. Replace the provisional triage criteria in Section 3 with the output. This session also produces D-02a (criteria confirmed objective) and D-02b (process for reviewing criteria over time).
Action 4 — Required before pilot launch
Validate request volume (D-04 / CA-04). Pull 24 months of Jira data across all six service desks. Validate the 150–300 request/year assumption. This provides the denominator for measuring pilot success and confirms the business case figures.
